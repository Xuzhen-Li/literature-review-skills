# Claim-strength examples

Use the ladder in `SKILL.md`. These are format examples, not facts you may paste into a manuscript.

Established, two rows, same direction:

> Field trials in two regions show a yield gain (Ada et al., 2019; E010; Berg, 2021; E011).

Supported, association only, because both rows are observational:

> The two cohorts indicate that exposure is associated with the outcome (Ada et al., 2019; E010; Berg, 2021; E011). The studies do not establish that the exposure causes the outcome.

Suggestive, one row:

> One greenhouse study suggests the effect may depend on temperature (Chen, 2022; E014). That result has not been repeated in the rows included here.

Contested:

> The direction of the effect is contested (Ada et al., 2019; E010; Berg, 2021; E011). Ada and colleagues reported a gain; Berg reported none in a different cultivar.

No row: do not write the sentence. If the outline claim requires it, mark the clause `[NEEDS SOURCE]` and stop. The citation checker will fail until the marker is gone.

Do not write `proves`, `conclusively`, `for the first time`, `we are the first`, `no study has`, `undoubtedly`, `clearly demonstrates`, `safe and effective`, or `paradigm shift` in a draft. `manuscript-polishing` treats those as block phrases.
